## Core Principle

Create a distilled editorial photograph with controlled optical imperfection. Preserve a small ranked semantic core, not every source detail. Every transformation must be physically motivated by the source image or explicitly requested by the user; physically motivated does not mean visually timid.

Treat composition, motion, optical layering, and lighting as independent diagnostic axes. Then choose exactly one primary mechanism and at most one support mechanism for the final art direction. The primary mechanism must dominate the image's light, movement, and palette; the support mechanism must not compete with it.

## Diagnostic Checklist

Before choosing a strategy, identify:

- **Hero:** the face, relationship, gesture, silhouette, object, flower, structure, or horizon that carries the meaning and must remain recognizable.
- **Hero invariants:** only the identity, count, anatomy, pose, clothing, interaction, essential shared object, product geometry, label, architecture, perspective, or native color required to recognize that hero.
- **Existing light:** flat, window, hard sun, backlit, mixed, or night.
- **Depth assets:** near foreground, middle-ground hero, distant background, glass, mirror, foliage, crowd, traffic, or architecture.
- **Motion assets:** walking, turning, reaching, wind, traffic, fabric, hair, hands, leaves, paper, water, or light.
- **Optical assets:** real flowers, foliage, glass, mirrors, windows, reflections, shallow depth, or a credible bright opening.
- **Risks:** identity drift, anatomy, repeated people, labels, architecture, perspective, or excessive blur.
- **Skin baseline:** when skin is visible, observe its source-relative complexion lightness, undertone, apparent age, and softness before applying any palette change.
- **Hero exposure baseline:** observe whether visible skin, one light garment plane, and one dark garment plane are readable in the source. Preserve that relative separation unless the source is already a silhouette or the user explicitly requests one.
- **Non-human hero exposure baseline:** for an object, flower, structure, or landscape, observe one readable source-relative midtone or material plane and keep it legible after grading.
- **Hero spatial axis:** determine whether the hero's defining direction is horizontal, vertical, or balanced before choosing the output ratio.
- **Mood and palette intent:** distinguish source-led color, summer breeze, golden memory, blue hour, night solitude, or another user-named direction. Do not infer nostalgia from backlight alone.
- **Architecture role:** classify architecture as a passage, standalone hero structure, or background context. Only passage architecture receives a path and vanishing-point preservation rule.
- **Implied movement:** a walkway, corridor, bridge, platform, repeated fixtures, receding railing, or other public passage whose perspective makes slow-shutter flow believable even when no person is visible.
- **Practical lights:** path lamps, edge lights, ground lights, signage, windows, or fixtures that can act as visible halation sources.

### Hero Precedence

When people carry the emotional meaning, their identity, relationship, gesture, and essential shared object outrank the location. Mountains, buildings, trees, horizon detail, and source aspect ratio remain flexible context unless the user explicitly makes the setting documentary or identity-sensitive.

Use a counterfactual test:

- if changing an element changes who the hero is or what the relationship/action means, make it an invariant;
- if changing it only makes the location less exact, fade it;
- if removing it does not affect the story, remove it.

Do not convert every visible structure or landscape edge into an invariant merely because preservation is possible.

## Ranked Preservation

Write preservation as a descending priority, not a flat lock list. In `dream-haze`, keep only two to four items at full fidelity.

1. the semantic hero or structural path;
2. the defining relationship, gesture, rhythm, or vanishing perspective;
3. one identity-bearing object, silhouette, or repeated sequence;
4. one native color only when it identifies the hero.

Everything below that threshold belongs in Fade or Remove. For an illuminated walkway, a useful ranking is `perspective lines > lamp sequence > warm/cool contrast > distant building outline`. The last item may disappear if it competes with the first three.

## Hero Sharpness Island

Preservation priority decides **what** remains; the hero sharpness island decides **where motion must stop**.

- Define one contiguous protected zone around the semantic hero plus a small safety margin.
- Keep the hero's defining contour and one material or identity texture readable inside it.
- Motion, diffusion, reflection, and halation may frame, pass behind, or originate beside the island, but must not sweep across its center.
- Keep the transition photographic: crisp anchor, short falloff, then atmospheric motion. Avoid a cutout or pasted-on edge.
- `45% kinetic atmosphere` is visual weight across the composition, not blur coverage over 45% of the hero.

For people, protect the face plus the full gesture or relationship silhouette. For products, protect the body, label, and functional edges. For a path, bridge, corridor, or architectural route, protect the central walking surface, inner railing boundaries, lamp cores, and one continuous repeated rhythm from foreground to vanishing point. Hero-surface texture must not be placed in Fade.

For visible skin, protect anatomical contours while keeping microtexture softly photographic. Do not make pores, wrinkles, veins, creases, age spots, or dry texture more visible than in the source.

Protect tonal readability as well as contour. A person who is readable in the source must not become a featureless or near-black silhouette merely because the background is brighter. Retain source-relative subject exposure, a readable skin base tone when visible, the identity of light clothing, and at least one dimensional plane in dark clothing. For a non-human hero, retain one readable source-relative midtone or material plane. Silhouette treatment is valid only when source-supported or explicitly requested.

## Human Skin Identity

Do not infer or relabel ethnicity from a face or hand. Preserve what is visually observable in the source:

- relative complexion lightness and translucency;
- warm, cool, neutral, olive, pink, golden, or other observed undertone;
- apparent age and degree of skin smoothness;
- natural contrast between skin, jewelry, clothing, and nearby objects.

Apply palette reconstruction to the environment first, then protect skin as a separate identity color family. Colored light may cast a soft environmental reflection, but it must not replace the base complexion. Do not tan, darken, redden, gray, desaturate, or age the person. Do not add gritty clarity, deeper creases, prominent veins, enlarged pores, rough dryness, or beauty-filter plasticity.

For a pale or light-complexioned source under night lighting, retain the light, soft, slightly luminous skin impression while allowing restrained blue or amber edge reflections. `Clear subject` means readable anatomy and gesture, not high-definition dermal texture.

## Semantic Compression

### Keep

Keep:

- one hero;
- one essential action, relationship, or object cue;
- one native accent color;
- zero to two context cues by default.

For emotional people photos, a bench, bicycle, instrument, bouquet, or other shared object may be essential. The surrounding mountain, building, tree line, field, or skyline usually remains context rather than an invariant.

### Fade

Convert selected secondary material into atmosphere:

- near-lens fabric, foliage, flowers, paper, shoulders, or object edges;
- midground secondary figures, furniture, vehicles, or architectural fragments;
- background light, color, traffic, foliage, or simplified structural trails.

Faded elements must lose small-detail readability while retaining direction, mass, and depth. Window grids, distinct building contours, individual branches or leaves, signs, paving texture, and distant objects should collapse into two to four broad motion or color masses. If a viewer can still inventory the Fade list, it has not faded enough.

### Remove

Remove:

- unrelated small objects;
- duplicate people or repeated props;
- competing signs or high-contrast clutter;
- secondary color accents that break the palette;
- any invented decorative object not supported by the source.

## Visual Allocation

Use approximate visual shares that do not overlap:

| Mode | Hero | Kinetic atmosphere | Quiet space |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Identity lock | 45% | 25% | 30% |
| Restrained premium | 40% | 30% | 30% |
| Dream haze | 30% | 45% | 25% |
| Experimental memory | 20% | 65% | 15% |

Treat these values as art-direction targets, not pixel measurements. Use identity-lock allocation whenever recognition-sensitive detail overrides the requested strength.

For `dream-haze`, the 45% kinetic share must read as a major compositional layer, not a few tiny streaks. Use broad directional foliage, field, fabric, light, color, or background motion while keeping the hero stable.

In `dream-haze`, build three source-supported motion-depth layers whenever the source contains foreground, midground, and background material:

1. near-lens directional veil;
2. midground secondary-form drag;
3. background light, color, or structural trails.

For each layer, state its source, direction, scale, and opacity or occlusion. Do not require all three when the source lacks the necessary material. Never invent people, flowers, glass, or props merely to fill a layer.

All three layers must also state their boundary relative to the hero sharpness island. Prefer large motion in the outer frame and background, moderate drag in secondary midground forms, and little or no motion inside the protected island.

## Composition Axis

Choose exactly one composition strategy.

### Source-Led Asymmetry

Default for most portraits, groups, flowers, landscapes, and street scenes.

- Preserve the believable source viewpoint.
- Reframe, crop edges, enlarge the hero, or move the hero off-center while preserving relative body positions and the essential relationship.
- Create one directional path from the hero into kinetic and quiet space.
- Keep no more than two context cues readable.
- Treat the source aspect ratio as flexible, but choose the new ratio from the hero's spatial direction. A horizontally spreading tree, bridge, flock, shoreline, or relationship may remain landscape; a vertical figure or corridor may use 4:5 or 3:4. Never force portrait solely because the source is wide or the hero is small.

### Geometry-Preserving Frame

Use only when a product, object, structure, label, landscape, or exact scene is the hero, or when the user requests identity lock or documentary fidelity. Do not select it just because an emotional people photo contains recognizable scenery.

- Preserve silhouette, proportions, perspective, label placement, and recognition-sensitive edges.
- Create atmosphere outside critical geometry.
- Prefer background simplification, passing light, and edge motion over viewpoint invention.
- Do not force a low angle or large crop that changes the object or structure.

### Low-Angle Color Field

Use only when an existing open sky, wall, or broad field and the source viewpoint can support it.

- Place the hero against a broad cobalt, cyan, ink-green, or charcoal field.
- Keep the result photographic, not graphic illustration.
- Add depth with an existing foreground edge or source-supported motion.
- Reject this strategy when it would distort anatomy, perspective, or source geometry.

## Motion Axis

Choose exactly one motion strategy.

### Crowd Drift

Use when visible people, walking, traffic, stations, streets, or the implied flow of an empty corridor, walkway, bridge, platform, or repeated perspective-light sequence makes slow-shutter movement plausible.

- Keep one hero or relationship stable.
- Drag existing passersby, sleeves, shoulders, vehicles, or, in an empty passage, existing lights, railings, and edge masses along the source perspective.
- Use believable slow-shutter separation, not radial software blur.
- On a walkway or corridor, keep the central path and primary lamp sequence stable. Confine long trails to outer rail zones, secondary light residuals, and background masses.
- Never invent people to illustrate movement. Do not apply to isolated products or scenes with no plausible passage or directional flow.

### Passing Gesture

Use when a real nearby hand, fabric edge, hair, paper, foliage, water, or light edge can carry movement.

- Keep the hero's face, key gesture, product edge, or flower center stable.
- Confine motion to one or two existing nearby elements.
- Do not invent a moving hand, curtain, petal, or prop that changes the story.

### Source-Layer Drift

Use as the safe fallback when no crowd or passing gesture is supported.

- Stretch existing background edges, architecture, foliage, color, reflections, or light.
- Preserve all critical subject geometry.
- Create directional atmosphere without adding new objects.
- Prefer this route for products, architecture, landscapes, flowers, and ambiguous still scenes.

## Optical Axis

Choose at most one optical layer.

### None

Use when the source has no credible floral or reflective optical material. Rely on source depth, motion, light bloom, and restrained diffusion.

### Floral Diffusion

Use only when flowers or foliage exist or are explicitly requested.

- Turn real near-lens blooms or leaves into color veils.
- Preserve at least one blossom center, petal edge, face plane, or hand gesture.
- Do not add absent flower species or random petals.

### Glass Memory

Use only when windows, mirrors, transit glass, reflective interiors, or visible reflections exist.

- Create one controlled reflection layer.
- Preserve perspective, window geometry, face count, and limb structure.
- Do not duplicate faces or use reflection as unexplained fantasy.

## Lighting Axis

Choose exactly one lighting treatment.

### Source-Light Bloom

Default for flat or mixed light.

- Preserve the observed light direction.
- Soften only credible highlights and lower digital micro-contrast.
- Do not invent a strong sun, window, or rim light.

### Backlit Halation

Use for existing backlight, window light, hard sun, rim light, a credible bright sky opening, or visible practical fixtures such as path, edge, or ground lamps. The source need not sit behind a person; a real fixture can drive local halation and light leakage.

- Bloom around real bright edges and light sources.
- Preserve a small luminous core and the identity of the fixture or rim edge. Halation is local glow around a readable source, not total source blur.
- Choose bloom color from the selected mood and the observed source. Honey-gold suits golden memory; summer daylight may use pale cream, clean cyan-white, or leaf-filtered light without an amber cast.
- Keep skin and materials dimensional; avoid global white fog.
- Keep the hero exposure floor independent from the bloom: backlight may outline the subject but must not crush a source-readable person into black.
- Keep flare, bokeh, and negative-space highlights optically irregular, asymmetrical, amorphous, and non-semantic. State this positive morphology in the generation brief without enumerating forbidden symbol names; use named examples only during result QA.

### Night Color Glow

Use for night scenes with existing practical lights.

- Stretch real distant lights into restrained trails.
- Use ink blue or deep cyan shadows with limited amber or coral warmth.
- Avoid cyberpunk neon unless the source already contains it.

## Primary and Support Route

The final brief must declare:

- **Primary mechanism:** the one effect responsible for most visible transformation;
- **Support mechanism:** zero or one secondary effect that strengthens depth or motion;
- **Physical reason:** which observed source asset makes both believable.

Axes diagnose materials; they do not impose equal weights. A night walkway with visible ground lamps can therefore use `Backlit Halation` as the primary mechanism and `Crowd Drift` as the support mechanism, even if no person is visible, because the fixtures are real sources and the receding passage implies movement.

## Sharpness Hierarchy

Use three levels:

1. **Anchor sharpness:** readable face plane, eye line, hand gesture, product contour, label, flower center, structure, or horizon. Human skin retains soft tonal transitions and source-level microtexture.
2. **Atmospheric softness:** clothing edges, hair, foliage, background, reflected light, or noncritical material.
3. **Directional motion:** only source-supported foreground, midground, or background forms.

Never blur the whole frame uniformly. A hero may be softened photographically but must remain decisively sharper and more structurally legible than its surrounding motion field.

Sharpness cannot compensate for underexposure. Preserve source-relative midtone separation inside the hero island; do not substitute a crisp black cutout for readable subject detail.

## Mood and Color Direction

- Re-author the palette visibly in `dream-haze`; source white balance is not an invariant.
- Choose one dominant environmental field, one luminous family, and neutral support. Their temperature relationship may be cool-warm, cool-cool, or warm-neutral when the source and mood support it; do not force the same palette recipe onto every photograph.
- Use approximate shares such as 60–75% dominant field, 15–30% luminous or contrasting family, and the remainder neutral support. These shares describe hierarchy, not a mandatory cool-field formula.
- Useful mood routes include: `summer-breeze` with airy blue, fresh green, and pale sunlit cream; `golden-memory` with honey gold, faded grass, warm cream, and cool shadow depth; `blue-hour` with cobalt, dusk blue, and silver-blue light without magenta contamination; `night-solitude` with ink blue or deep cyan plus restrained amber or coral; and `source-led` with a visibly distilled version of observed source colors.
- Keep lighting mechanism and palette selection separate. Backlit halation describes how light behaves, not whether the whole image becomes sunset-colored.
- Preserve at most one native source color as an identity cue in addition to protected human skin.
- Human skin is exempt from aggressive palette replacement: preserve its source-relative brightness, undertone, and age impression while placing environmental color in rim light, reflection, or surrounding shadow.
- Reduce miscellaneous hues and source-local color noise instead of merely increasing saturation.
- Keep shadows chromatic and avoid crushed neutral black, global white haze, or a source-like color balance that makes the edit read as a filter.

## Analog Texture

Use restrained:

- fine-to-medium film grain;
- lowered micro-contrast;
- softened digital edge acuity;
- local halation;
- mild chromatic aberration near high-contrast edges;
- slight color-registration imperfection;
- subtle scan texture and vignette.

Do not use heavy scratches, grunge overlays, giant fake light leaks, plastic skin, crushed blacks, or oversharpened film presets.

## Strength Translation

### Restrained Premium

Use subtle motion, one restrained optical or lighting cue, and strong geometry or identity preservation. Default for products, brands, architecture, and recognition-sensitive portraits.

### Dream Haze

Use visibly directional atmosphere while preserving one stable hero. Default for an unqualified request to dreamify a personal photo, social image, or editorial portrait.

- Make the kinetic layer approximately 45% of the visual weight and clearly visible at first glance.
- Concentrate that kinetic weight outside the hero sharpness island; preserve the hero's defining edges and one surface texture.
- Permit editorial reframing and aspect-ratio changes unless explicitly forbidden.
- Use broad motion masses, luminous flow, or depth-separated drag rather than a warm grade plus global softness.
- Rebuild the chosen mood-appropriate color relationship so the result is immediately different from the source palette without drifting into an unrequested sunset, memory, magenta, or purple cast.
- Make Fade elements lose readable microstructure; retain only their directional mass and color contribution.
- Do not describe the main transformation mainly with `subtle`, `gentle`, `mild`, or `restrained`; reserve those words for grain, aberration, vignette, and other supporting texture.

### Experimental Memory

Use stronger drag, reflection, refraction, doubling, or color contamination while preserving one unmistakable anchor. Do not allow semantic collapse or arbitrary scene invention.

## Poster Treatment

Only add poster copy or typography when explicitly requested within a photo-dreamification task.

- Use one title of one to six words.
- Keep typography under 12% of the frame.
- Place type in existing quiet space.
- Do not cover faces, gestures, labels, or critical geometry.
- Recommend adding exact long text in a design tool when spelling matters.
